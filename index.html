🪐 Meet the Solar System!

☀️ The Solar System

Our Solar System has one Sun at the centre — a giant ball of burning gas! Eight planets orbit (travel around) the Sun in oval-shaped paths called orbits. The planets in order from the Sun are: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

🌟 Fun Fact: It takes Earth 365¼ days to travel once around the Sun — that's one year! Mercury is the fastest: it whizzes round in just 88 days!
🌍 The Moon

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite — it orbits around the Earth, not the Sun directly. The Moon takes about 27–29 days to travel once around the Earth. That's roughly one month! The Moon does not make its own light — we see it because it reflects sunlight.

🌙 Fun Fact: The Moon is about 384,400 km away from Earth. If you could drive there by car, it would take about 150 days!
🔵 Spherical Bodies

The Sun, Earth, and Moon are all approximately spherical — that means ball-shaped! Gravity pulls everything towards the centre, which is why planets form a round shape. They're not perfectly smooth spheres though — Earth has mountains, oceans and is slightly squashed at the poles.

⚽ Think about it: Gravity is like invisible glue pulling everything to the centre — just like how a ball of clay squishes into a round shape when you squeeze it from all sides!
🌅 Day, Night & the Sun's Journey

The Earth spins (rotates) on its axis — an imaginary line through the North and South Poles. It spins once every 24 hours — that gives us day and night! The side facing the Sun gets day, while the side facing away gets night. Because Earth is spinning west-to-east, the Sun appears to rise in the east in the morning, move across the sky, and set in the west in the evening — even though it's actually Earth that's moving!

🌄 Remember: The Sun doesn't really move across the sky — WE move! Earth is spinning, so it looks like the Sun is travelling from east to west every day.
🪐 Solar System: Watch the planets orbit the Sun! The closer a planet is to the Sun, the faster it travels. Notice how the inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) move much quicker than the giant outer planets.
